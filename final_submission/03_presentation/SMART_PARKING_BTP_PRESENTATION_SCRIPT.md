### Slide 2: The Urban Parking Problem (0:35 – 1:15)
*"To understand the motivation behind this work, consider the daily challenge in metropolitan city centers. Empirical studies by Professor Donald Shoup establish that between 30% and 50% of urban traffic congestion in downtown corridors is generated by drivers actively cruising for curbside parking. In dense commercial districts, arriving vehicles repeatedly circle blocks searching for a spotted bay. This cruising behavior leads to immense economic waste, millions of excess vehicle-kilometers traveled, and elevated CO2 and particulate emissions. Crucially, passive systems like static signs or naive smartphone apps fail because they cause a 'herding effect'—directing dozens of drivers toward the same single spotted vacancy."*

### Slide 3: Research Motivation & Vision (1:15 – 1:55)
*"Our vision shifts urban parking from passive monitoring to active, coordinated multi-agent allocation. Urban road networks are inherently non-Euclidean graphs where travel delay depends on road geometry and turn restrictions. Graph Attention Networks allow parking facilities to dynamically share congestion pressure. By treating each parking facility as an autonomous agent in a Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning framework, we can coordinate driver allocations across competing facilities, preventing localized bottlenecks before they form."*

### Slide 5: Literature Review & Foundations (2:35 – 3:15)
*"Our theoretical framework rests on three pillars:  
In traditional allocation, heuristics like First-In-First-Out and Service-in-Random-Order provide simple greedy queue baselines, but lack predictive network-wide foresight.  
In spatial learning, Graph Attention Networks introduce learnable self-attention coefficients to weigh neighboring facilities dynamically.  
In reinforcement learning, Multi-Agent DDPG under Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution allows offline centralized evaluation while decentralized actors execute sub-millisecond edge inferences."*

---

### Slide 6: Identified Research Gaps (3:15 – 3:55)
*"While prior literature introduced the MARL-GCN concept, critical scientific and practical gaps remained.  
First, unconstrained MADDPG critics in continuous parking environments experience severe overestimation bias, causing critic loss to explode by 10 orders of magnitude.  
Second, standard single-step replay violates Markovian assumptions when training recurrent GRUs.  
Third, previous work lacked rigorous hypothesis testing with Holm--Bonferroni corrections against greedy baselines.  
Fourth, no production bridge existed between offline simulation scripts and real-time mobile applications with database concurrency."*

### Slide 7: Proposed System Overview (3:55 – 4:35)
*"Our proposed system provides an end-to-end multi-tiered pipeline:  
Drivers interact via a React Native Expo mobile app. The mobile client calls a Flask REST microservice that acts as an authoritative decision gate. The backend queries real-time IoT sensor arrays from Firebase RTDB, formats the state into normalized graph tensors via our Campus Adapter, and passes it to the PyTorch neural engine. If the model is disabled or unavailable, a deterministic heuristic fallback guarantees 100% service uptime."*

### Slide 8: Backend Architecture & Concurrency Gates (4:35 – 5:15)
*"Security and operational reliability are baked into the backend architecture. Before any neural pass occurs, four sequential gates are evaluated: startup pre-warming, dynamic feature-flag gating, strict 5-stall topology validation, and defensive action masking.  
Furthermore, our booking endpoint implements dual-layer concurrency protection: an in-process mutex serializes local request threads, while a fail-closed Firebase ETag check with conditional HTTP If-Match PUT completely eliminates double-booking race conditions."*

### Slide 9: Graph Representation of Parking Networks (5:15 – 5:50)
*"We formulate the municipal parking network as a heterogeneous graph G = (V, E, W). Each facility is represented by an 8-dimensional node feature tensor encoding: real-time occupancy ratio, capacity, baseline vacant cruising delay, localized cruising density, arrival demand forecasts, pedestrian walking distance, and normalized physical coordinates. Graph edges capture road connectivity weighted by inverse physical travel distance."*

---

### Slide 10: Two-Stage Graph Attention Network (5:50 – 6:25)
*"Spatial feature extraction is executed in two stages:  
Stage 1 computes intra-partition self-attention between connected facilities within localized clusters, masking invalid topological edges with -9x10^15 before softmax.  
Stage 2 projects aggregated features through an inter-partition linear transformation with ELU non-linearity, capturing regional competition across distinct municipal districts."*

---

### Slide 11: Temporal Recurrence & Policy Hypernetwork (6:25 – 7:05)
*"Temporal memory is maintained by an Agent Temporal GRU that updates a 64-dimensional temporal hidden state based on current spatial features and previous actions.  
Rather than learning fixed static actor weights, a Policy Hypernetwork dynamically generates personalized layer weights W_G and bias b_G conditioned on the facility's temporal congestion state. Decentralized actors then execute continuous allocation priorities in [0, 1]. In production, stateless serving resets h_0 to zero for independent driver requests, eliminating state drift."*

### Slide 12: Eliminating Bellman Divergence: Twin Critics (7:05 – 7:50)
*"[Referencing Fig. 1 on Slide]  
This slide highlights our primary theoretical contribution. As seen in the left plot, unconstrained single-critic MADDPG suffered catastrophic Bellman loss explosion: critic MSE exploded from 31 to 5.91x10^11 by episode 15, crashing training.  
We eliminated this divergence by introducing four stabilization interventions: Twin Critics with Clipped Double-Q updates based on the TD3 principle, reward scaling by 0.1x, gradient norm clipping at 5.0, and feature detachment. As shown by the green curve, critic loss remained bounded at <= 4.04 across all 30 episodes, while Q-values calibrated stably at 11.98."*

### Slide 15: Empirical Benchmark Findings (9:00 – 9:45)

The benchmark results yield key scientific findings:

### Slide 16: Architectural Ablations & Module Attribution (9:45 – 10:30)
*"[Referencing Fig. 3 on Slide]  
Our controlled ablation study across 6 variants (N=5 seeds) produced vital insights:  
Most crucially, removing the temporal GRU caused a catastrophic +545.5% surge in cruising delay—jumping from 4.64 minutes to 29.93 minutes—with extreme statistical significance (p = 3.03x10^-6). This proves that temporal historical memory is the single indispensable component.  
Conversely, disabling spatial attention (No-GAT) descriptively reduced delay by 23%. On a compact 9-node macro-graph, attention matrices introduce optimization overhead during early training, allowing simpler MLP projections to learn faster."*

### Slide 18: Concurrency Hardening & Booking Atomicity (11:10 – 11:45)
*"In a smart parking system, double-booking is a critical operational failure.  
We eliminated this risk by implementing fail-closed optimistic concurrency control: the backend queries the ETag from Firebase RTDB and sends a conditional PUT with the If-Match header. If two drivers race for the last stall, the first succeeds, while the second receives an HTTP 412 from Firebase, which our API maps to an HTTP 409 Conflict. Furthermore, failed database writes return HTTP 502/504—never false 200 OK confirmations."*

### Slide 20: Limitations & Threats to Validity (12:20 – 12:55)
*"In keeping with rigorous academic integrity, we openly disclose the limitations of this study:

Third, continuous actor logits can suffer from policy over-smoothing in uniform demand, favoring discrete greedy heuristics."*
